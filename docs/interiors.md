# Interiors

Hollow blocks and what is inside them: light, furniture, doors, fireplaces, stairs. Interiors are meant to be seen from the street.

- **Hollow blocks**: `Wall`, `WallWithWindow` and `Foundation` cells (`isHollowType()` in `cellUtils.ts`) are masonry shells, not solids: `hollowShellGeo(radii, hollow, openings)` in `geometryCache.ts` = `shapedBoxGeo` − inner volume − openings, computed with `three-bvh-csg` once per combination (`three-bvh-csg` ≥ 0.0.18 needs `three-mesh-bvh` ≥ 0.9, a direct dependency, while drei keeps its own nested 0.8 copy; npm cannot resolve both bumps in one `npm install`: install `three-mesh-bvh` first, then `three-bvh-csg`). `getHollowShape()` gives `CellContext.hollow` (null for roofs/arches): each side is `exterior` (wall, openings allowed), `open` (hollow neighbour → no wall, one continuous interior per building) or `partition` (roof/arch neighbour → blind wall); a pillar is kept at an inner corner whose two sides are open but whose diagonal is not hollow (L-shaped buildings), otherwise the neighbours' outer walls leave a hole; floor **and** ceiling `FLOOR_T` in every block, even under another hollow block (2 × `FLOOR_T` slab between storeys): all ceilings of a storey are at the same height. An open ceiling under storeys and a closed one under roofs left a `FLOOR_T` step where the edges of abutting roofs (eaves, panels) hung down; for the same reason `gableRoofParts` drops the eave fascia on a side that butts against a neighbour (`eavePos`/`eaveNeg`). Interiors are finished by `interiorParts()` ([src/render/cells/interiorParts.ts](../src/render/cells/interiorParts.ts), called from `shellParts`): wooden floorboards with seams every 0.25, a plastered ceiling and one beam per cell. Floor and ceiling use `hollowShellGeo`'s sibling `hollowSheetGeo()`, which follows the same inner contour (rounded towers, corner pillars) and stops exactly at the cell edge on `open` sides so neighbouring sheets meet without overlap. The shell body is the only part with `doubleSide: true` in its `MaterialSpec` (its own merged group, `DoubleSide`, `shadowSide: BackSide` so shadows are unchanged): walls and floors are thinner than the camera's near plane, so a camera inside them would otherwise see through. Walls are `WALL_T` thick and follow the rounded contour (inner radius = r − `WALL_T`). **Rounded junctions** ("interior corner roundness", `connectedWallInteriorRadius`): `getCornerRadii()` gives that radius only to a corner on a straight facade — exactly one side neighbour and the diagonal empty, a rule symmetric between the two cells; corners inside a block or at a re-entrant angle stay sharp (rounded, they bored a shaft through the floors in the middle of a block). Such a corner makes a groove in the facade down to the neighbour, across an `open` side: `innerSectionXZ` keeps a `WALL_T` wall along it (inner arc, then a quarter circle round the bottom of the groove) and only meets the neighbour beyond — a square cavity there cut through the wall and showed the floors (user report at 0.5). When the grooves of both corners of an open side leave less than `MIN_PASSAGE`, that side is walled. `interiorParts` measures beam, joist and seam lengths on that same contour (`innerReach`), and `masonryParts` faces the whole arc of a rounded corner when the adjacent face has no facing because a block stands in front (`wholeArc`: the groove's back stayed bare). Tested in `hollowShell.test.ts` and `buildVillage.test.ts` (no room part pokes out of its cell's rounded contour). Windows, doors and arrow slits are real holes (`OPENING_SIZES`): glass, door panel and slit back are set inside the wall thickness, frames stay on the outer face with a hole aligned on the opening. Openings are cut with **square** boxes: a rounded cutter blew a 4-window shell up to ~14 k vertices (CSG triangle splitting), vs ~1.6 k (budget tested in `hollowShell.test.ts`).
- **Window glow & interiors**: parts with `glow: true` in their `MaterialSpec` (window glass, arrow slits) form their own merged group whose emissive is driven by `windowGlow` in `VillageMeshes` — imperatively, then `invalidate()`. Window glass is clear and light (`WINDOW_GLASS_OPACITY` 0.3 in `wallWindowCellParts.ts`), day and night, because interiors are meant to be seen from the street (future interior furniture — never hide or cull interiors); its emissive is divided by its opacity so that, once alpha-blended, the glow is `GLASS_GLOW_INTENSITY` (a light warm veil, the room stays visible). **Interior light**: the walls block the sun, so rooms are lit by a fake lamp in the shader (`injectInterior()` in [src/render/growMaterial.ts](../src/render/growMaterial.ts), main material only, not the depth material), placed at the flames of the room's chandelier (`INTERIOR_LAMP_Y`, `chandelierParts()` in `interiorParts.ts`: chain, iron ring, 4 candles; flames are `glow` parts with a dark wick colour, so they only light up with `windowGlow`). **One chandelier per room, not per cell**: `assignRoomLamps()` ([src/utils/roomLamps.ts](../src/utils/roomLamps.ts)) flood-fills rooms (hollow cells of one storey, 4-connected — the same cells whose shared side is `open`), hangs the first chandelier in the most central cell and adds one only for cells more than `LAMP_REACH` (2, Chebyshev) cells from every chandelier, so up to 5 × 5 a room has one; each cell is lit by the nearest one (`CellContext.chandelier` says whether the cell carries it). The shader light falls off horizontally (`LAMP_FALLOFF`, half at 2 cells). Lit parts are **explicitly marked** with `Part.interior` (`InteriorKind`, `asInterior()` in `parts.ts`), never guessed from position — stones embedded in the wall used to catch the light on their edges: `'room'` = furnishings (floor, ceiling, beam, chandelier, future furniture: mark them `'room'`), every face turned towards the lamp is lit; `'shell'` = the hollow shell body, lit only inside the cavity: between floor and ceiling, and on a vertical face whose horizontal normal does not point away from the cell axis by at least `EXTERIOR_SUPPORT` (0.45: `dot(n.xz, p.xz)` — the outer contour is convex and contains the circle of radius 0.5, cavity faces look towards the axis, reveals and stairwell edges are much closer). That criterion does not depend on where along the wall the face is (testing |x| and |z| together once left a dark 8 cm strip of interior wall on each side of every seam between two cells of a room, where the wall runs to the cell edge along an `open` side), lights window reveals up to the outer face, like light spilling through the glass, and never takes the back of a rounded junction's groove for the room — the former test (distance to the axis along the normal < 0.5 − `WALL_T`) did, and lit and plastered it behind the stones (a yellow band on the facade). The mortar tint of `VC_MASONRY` uses the same criterion (`VC_EXTERIOR_SUPPORT`; tested against the real shell faces in `hollowShell.test.ts`). Stones, quoins, frames, glass, flames: unmarked, never lit. The mode (0 none, 1 shell, 2 room — only in hollow cells) is stored in `aCell.w` = code × 4 + mode, code = fall × 100 when mode is 0 (only roofs fall), else the offset to the room's chandelier cell, `(dx + 16) × 32 + (dz + 16)`; all of it is hashed by `VillageChunkCache` (adding a cell to a room re-merges the chunks of the whole room). The fragment adds `albedo × uInteriorLight × max(dot(normal, toLamp), 0)` to its emissive — computed **per fragment** (the vertex shader only passes cell-local position, lamp, normal and mode): interpolated per vertex, it followed the large irregular triangles CSG leaves on the shell walls and drew light bands across interior walls. `VillageMeshes` sets `uInteriorLight` (shared uniform object per material set): `interiorFill` (store, neutral day fill) fading into a warm lamp as `windowGlow` rises. A chandelier costs ~250 vertices. **Furniture**: `furnitureParts(ctx, openings)` ([src/render/cells/furnitureParts.ts](../src/render/cells/furnitureParts.ts), called from `interiorParts`, which receives the cell's openings from `shellParts`) — deterministic per cell (hash of coordinates): under the room's chandelier a table + benches (ground floor) or a rug (upper floors); doors and fireplaces are planned **per room** (chandelier zone, `lampZones()`) before any wall is built, by `buildVillage` between computing all `CellContext`s and running the builders. **Doors**: `planDoors()` ([src/render/cells/openings.ts](../src/render/cells/openings.ts), result in `CellContext.doorFace`) — one per ground-floor zone, not one per cell (a row of merged houses used to show a door on every cell), on the exposed face of a `WallWithWindow` cell nearest the chandelier, a flat wall preferred over a rounded corner, never on a rampart; tested. The door panel and its ironwork are **moving parts** (`Part.door`), rendered as their own small groups and swung open inwards by the page when the walker comes near — hinge side, opening width, steps and swing in [src/render/cells/doors.ts](../src/render/cells/doors.ts), see [walk.md](walk.md); new door hardware must be marked the same way or it stays behind when the door opens. **Fireplaces**: `planFireplaces()` (`furnitureParts.ts`, result in `CellContext.fireplaceFace`), `FIREPLACE_CHANCE` of zones (~75 % ground floor, ~45 % upstairs in practice), on the zone cell nearest the chandelier whose wall is not `open`, not a door or slit, not in a rounded corner, and whose column is topped by a gable roof; any of the 4 walls — a ceiling beam that ends on that wall stops against the hood (`HOOD_REACH`, `interiorParts`; only accepting walls parallel to the beam left a 1-cell-wide row with nothing but its pierced gable walls); a blind wall is preferred, else a window wall **loses its window** (`openingKindsOf(ctx, fireplaceFace)` in `openings.ts`, shared by `wallWindowCellParts` and the planner) as long as the room keeps another window. **Flues**: roof chimney stacks are no longer random — `buildVillage` gives the roof cell at the top of each fireplace's column a flue (`CellContext.flues`, cell coordinates, `FLUE_OFFSET` from the centre towards the fireplace wall, deduplicated for stacked fireplaces), and `gableRoofParts({ flues })` draws a stack there, from under the pan to above the ridge (tested: one stack per fireplace column/wall, none without a fireplace). Accepting blind walls only gave 0 fireplaces on ground floors: every exterior face there has a door or a window. A fireplace is centred on its wall and takes its two corners; embers are a `glow` part; against walls, one slot per corner (`SLOT_OFFSET` along the wall, `WALL_GAP` from it): chests, cupboards, barrels, shelves with books and pots, workbenches (ground floor), one bed per cell upstairs (a bed spans the room, so it takes both corners on its side), shelves, or nothing. Never against an `open` side or the wall of a door; under a window or slit only pieces lower than the sill (`LOW_MAX_H`); no wall pieces in towers (rounded contour) or in a rounded corner. Scale: pieces are drawn for a 2.8 m cell (`DESIGN_CELL_METERS`) and placed with `HUMAN_SCALE` (`humanSized()`), so they keep their real size whatever `CELL_METERS` is; the fireplace is the exception — it reaches the ceiling and its hood lines up with the roof flue, so it follows the cell. Boxes and 8-segment cylinders only (~24 vertices per welded box); with chandeliers a generated 12-grid village is ~400 k vertices. `furnitureParts.test.ts` intercepts the real calls during `buildVillage` (`vi.mock`) to check, over generated villages: inside the cavity and rounded corners, door passage clear, nothing above the sill in front of a window, nothing against open sides, vertex budget, determinism. New furniture: add a `Piece` builder (back against +z, standing on y = 0) and pick it in the slot loop; `place()` makes every piece a walk-mode obstacle (`asObstacle`, its bounding box — see [walk.md](walk.md)). Bloom's threshold (0.85) is set so that only those lit windows bloom.
- **Interior decor**: ground floors are flagged in stone, upper floors boarded; four ceiling joists per cell run across the beam (`interiorParts.ts`). Windows get drawn-back curtains on the room side (`curtainParts` in `wallWindowCellParts.ts`, marked `'room'`; the first fold bites 4 cm into the opening — fully beside it, the wall hid them from the street; one colour per house). Furniture adds: tableware on the dining table, a writing desk upstairs, tapestries on blind walls, a cauldron, flames (`glow`) and a log pile at the fireplace, a pillow and footboard on beds. **Any `glow` part emitted by `furnitureParts` means a fireplace** (the test relies on it — the table candle is unlit), and nothing may stand in a door's passage (stools at the table ends were removed for that). The room lamp has its own paler colour (`INTERIOR_LAMP_COLOR`) and the glass veil is light (`GLASS_GLOW_INTENSITY` 0.1): with the saturated window-glow orange, every interior read as one flat amber surface at night.
- **Interior stairs**: every storey is linked to the one above, up to the attic. [src/render/cells/stairPlan.ts](../src/render/cells/stairPlan.ts) plans them (pure, tested in `stairs.test.ts`), [src/render/cells/stairParts.ts](../src/render/cells/stairParts.ts) builds them. `planStairs()` runs in `buildVillage` **after `planDoors` and before `planFireplaces`** (stairs are mandatory, fireplaces step aside), room by room bottom-up (`findRooms()` in `roomLamps.ts`, shared with the chandeliers): for each room of the storey above standing on a room, one stair, in a cell of the lower room right under it — an **L** in houses (lower flight of 6 solid boxed steps along one wall, a quarter turn of 3 winders round a newel in the corner, upper flight of 6 treads + risers on a stringer along the next wall; 0.3 wide (84 cm at 2.8 m, 1 m at 3.36 m): at 0.2 the user found it too narrow, and a flat landing at that width left 17 cm treads), a **spiral** in tower columns (`ctx.isIsolated`: stone wedges round a newel, 15 × 18° per storey, the same start angle for the whole column, its 90° landing facing the door or the open side); a room with no room above gets a **ladder** (miller's ladder against a blind wall, or beside a window, never in front of one) up to a hatch — open into a gable attic, closed by an oak lid under a spire, a crenellated top or a wall-walk. One storey is exactly one unit floor to floor; `STAIR_RISERS` derives from the cell size (~17.5 cm risers, `TARGET_RISE`: 16 for a 2.8 m cell, 20 for 3.36 m), the L flights (`L_FLIGHT` treads each) and the spiral's step count/angle follow, and the L tread shortens if needed to fit between the walls (`config/scale.test.ts`). Each plan carries its `opening` (stairwell polygon in cell XZ, edges tagged wall face / `free` / `arrival`), `footprint` and `lowFootprint` (what must stay out of the door passage); the L layouts are the same 8 for every cell, precomputed (`L_PLANS`) with their fit test memoized per cell shape. Results: `CellContext.stair` (leaving the cell) and `CellContext.stairBelow` (arriving through its floor). The opening is cut through the lower cell's ceiling slab and the upper cell's floor slab (`hollowShellGeo(…, holes)`, a CSG prism stopping 3 mm short of the walls — never coplanar with them) and through the floorboard / plaster sheets (`hollowSheetGeo(…, hole)`); `interiorParts` clips floor seams, the beam and joists around it (`clippedRun`, `outsideSpansOfBeam` in [src/utils/polygon.ts](../src/utils/polygon.ts)), lays trimmers along its free edges under the ceiling, and a balustrade on the upper floor (not on the arrival edge, nor on a spiral landing's edge when the next spiral starts there). Spiral cells have no beam or joists (the newel carries the floor) and, if they hold the room's chandelier, a lantern hangs from the newel instead (`spiralLanternParts`, flame at `INTERIOR_LAMP_Y`; the lamp light stays at the cell centre). Furniture, the table / rug and fireplaces avoid `stairObstacles(ctx)` (footprint, opening, stairwell + `RAILING_MARGIN`). Stair parts are `'room'` parts that legitimately rise above the cell's ceiling through the stairwell. Cost: ~3.5 ms of planning and +3 % vertices on a 12-grid village.
