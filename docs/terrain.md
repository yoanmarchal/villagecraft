# Sloping terrain

The ground under the village, building levels, and what follows the slope.

- **Sloping terrain**: the ground under the village is not flat. [src/render/relief.ts](../src/render/relief.ts) (pure, tested): `reliefHeight(x, z, relief)` is a sum of three sine waves in world space × `terrainRelief` (store, 0 = no waves), 0 at the grid centre (the orbit target stays on the ground) and faded out a few units past the grid (`reliefFor(gridSize, amplitude, shape)`), well before the hills, plus the hand sculpt. Sines, not noise, so that the exact same formula exists in GLSL (`RELIEF_GLSL`; uniforms in [src/render/reliefUniforms.ts](../src/render/reliefUniforms.ts), one set shared by the village materials) — change both together. **Terrain shape** ([src/terrainShape.ts](../src/terrainShape.ts), an immutable `{ seed, sculpt }` held by `VillageGrid.terrain`: it belongs to the village — saved, shared, undone — not to the settings): `seed` picks the waves' directions, phases, lengths and amplitudes (`wavesFor()`; `generateTerrain()` draws a new one and drops the sculpt, seed 0 = the original waves, which villages saved before seeds keep); `sculpt` is a sparse list of `[x, z, steps]` per grid cell (±`SCULPT_MAX_STEPS`), edited by the toolbar's raise / lower tools (`gridControllerStore.sculpt()`; right click does the opposite). Each sculpted cell carries a cubic B-spline bump (`SCULPT_STEP_HEIGHT` per step on a plateau, spilling two cells out), baked once per relief into a 0.25 lattice (`SculptField`) that JS reads bilinearly and the shader through a half-float texture (`uSculpt`, sampled with `textureLod` — keep it derivative-free). Never test `relief.amplitude` to know whether the ground is flat: use `isFlatRelief()` (a sculpted terrain with amplitude 0 is not flat), `reliefBound()` for its highest point and `reliefReach()` for its extent. **Buildings never lean and are never split in height**: [src/render/ground.ts](../src/render/ground.ts) `createGround()` gives every 4-connected group of built columns (arches included — they tie their two supports) one level, `baseAt(x, z)` = the highest terrain point under the group's footprint, rounded up to 0.01 — so the whole neighbour logic (shared rooms, ridges, ramparts, arches, masonry in grid coordinates) still reasons on a regular grid and there is never grass inside a room. Joining two buildings with a block therefore moves one of them to the common level; that is expected. `buildVillage(…, { width, depth, relief })` (`relief`: a `Relief`, or a plain amplitude in tests) adds that level to every cell's world position (never bake it into `toWorldPosition`, which stays the flat grid mapping) and exposes the terrain to builders as `CellContext.groundAt(lx, lz)` (cell-local, null on flat ground: builders then do exactly what they did before — keep it so, the flat path is what most tests cover). What follows the slope: `plinthParts()` ([src/render/cells/plinthParts.ts](../src/render/cells/plinthParts.ts)) extends ground-floor walls down to the terrain on the downhill side (same contour, same `masonryParts()` facing through `yRange`, so courses continue, and the corner quoin chains with them); door steps (`doorStepParts` in `wallWindowCellParts.ts`) descend from a threshold that stands above the ground, and `planDoors()` prefers the uphill face (`doorDrop()`); cobbles and their bedding are laid on the slope with `drapeMatrix()` ([src/render/cells/drape.ts](../src/render/cells/drape.ts) — a cobble is rotated about the centre of the WHOLE cobble so its two halves, drawn by two cells, match; the bedding is cut in 3 × 3 slabs); props stay plumb at the terrain height; trees use `groundHeight()`. Scene side (`VoxelScene.tsx`): `Terrain.tsx` uses a fine 0.25 mesh under the village (`TERRAIN_FINE_STEP`; things are placed at the analytic height, the drawn surface must not drift from it), built by [src/render/terrainMesh.ts](../src/render/terrainMesh.ts): the mesh and the distant hills depend only on the scene size (`terrainMesh()`), and a new relief only rewrites the heights and normals of the fine area (`applyRelief()`, same sums as `computeVertexNormals`; `terrainMesh.test.ts` checks it against a full rebuild) — rebuilding the whole mesh up to the horizon took ~90 ms on every generation, `GridLines` is draped on that same lattice, `CameraFloor` keeps the camera above the ground, and `PlacementPreview` adds `ground.baseAt()` and, with the terrain tools, drapes a patch on the targeted cell.
