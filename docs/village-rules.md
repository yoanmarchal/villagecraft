# Village rules

The grid and the procedural rules that turn placed blocks into houses, towers, ramparts, roofs, arches and streets.

- **Grid state & procedural generation**: [src/villageGrid.ts](../src/villageGrid.ts) owns the 3D `GridCell[][][]` and `recomputeProceduralLogic()`, which assigns each cell's `BlockType` (`Empty`, `Foundation`, `Wall`, `WallWithWindow`, `Roof`, `Arch`) by inspecting neighbours. **Always go through this recompute after any grid mutation** — never patch `cell.type` directly. The grid instance lives in `App`'s `useState`, so a hot update would keep running the old rules: `villageGrid.ts` ends with `import.meta.hot?.accept(() => window.location.reload())` — keep it.
- **Neighbour/shape queries**: [src/utils/cellUtils.ts](../src/utils/cellUtils.ts) — always query neighbours via `getCell()`/`hasOccupiedCell()` on a `CellLookup`, never index the raw grid array directly. The lookup is rebuilt per recompute; don't cache it across mutations.
- **Grid is pure occupancy + shape**: `VillageGrid` knows nothing about colors or the UI store. It also carries the village's terrain shape (`grid.terrain`, see **Sloping terrain**). It persists via `exportBlocks()`/`importBlocks()` (real blocks in placement order, auto-roof caps excluded) and `terrain`/`setTerrain()` — used by [src/store/villageStorage.ts](../src/store/villageStorage.ts) (localStorage) and by grid resizing in `App.tsx`.
- **Adding a new `BlockType`**: add the enum value in [src/types.ts](../src/types.ts), create a parts builder under `src/render/cells/` (return `Part[]` with cell-local matrices; use `geometryCache` helpers), dispatch it in `buildVillage.ts`, and extend `recomputeProceduralLogic()` in `villageGrid.ts` to assign it.
- **Defensive tops (towers, curtain walls)**: `roofCellParts` picks the top of a column from its context, without new `BlockType`s. Isolated roof → tower: slate roof following the tower's own contour (`spireBandGeo`/`towerSoffitGeo`: flared eave then steep cone, colour `spireColor`), or, for ~1 tower in 3 chosen by a hash of its column, a crenellated top with no roof. `isRampart()` in `cellUtils.ts` detects curtain walls (1-cell-thick straight run whose both ends butt against taller columns) → wall-walk + crenellated parapets with arrow-slit merlons instead of a gable roof; `CellContext.isRampart` is also true for the walls below, which get arrow slits and no door. Merlons (`merlonCount` for towers — rounded to a multiple of 4 and laid out symmetrically from the middle of each side, never on the rounded corners where they read as 45° chevrons; `merlonR`, `merlonH`) are rectangular stone, never on a roofed tower — that's how they're used on real enceintes.
- **Roof ridges & junctions**: a gable roof's ridge follows the building, not the cell: `getRidgeAxis()` (`cellUtils.ts`) takes the axis of the longest run of contiguous gable-roof cells (`isGableRoofCell()`: same level, not a spire, not a rampart) through the cell — so ridges are parallel inside a block (valleys between rows) and follow a 1-cell wing; on a tie (square block…) the whole group of contiguous gable-roof cells decides at once — its larger extent, else Z — never cell by cell (a cell that also touched a taller wall used to take the other axis and cross its ridge mid-block); a lone roof cell keeps the axis of its occupied neighbours. A wing meeting another roof at right angles used to butt its open end (a full-height triangle) against that cell's descending pan — a hole. Now a cell adds a half-roof (`gableRoofParts(…, { extent: 'pos' | 'neg' })`, from its centre to that side, no gable) toward each perpendicular neighbour **whose ridge points at it**: T junction, L corner with a single valley, crossing. Parallel neighbours never trigger it — an earlier version merged every cell with neighbours on both axes and crossed ridges all over wide blocks. Eave fascias stay only on sides with no neighbour; a gable's bottom band overhangs like them on a free side and stops at the cell edge next to a neighbour, 4 mm in front of the eave fascias (face at 0.544), so it covers the ends of the pans and side fascias (all at ±0.54) instead of sharing their plane — when coplanar, the pan's bevelled corner pierced it as a light spike at every gable corner; eave fascias are exactly as long as the pans (6 cm longer, they stuck out at every gable corner). At an L corner, the half-roof's pan facing the main roof's gable stops inside the gable's thickness (`panRunPos`/`panRunNeg`) and drops its fascia — it used to run past the gable's foot to the facade, battens and fascia included. Tested by vertical rays across junction boundaries, in a 4 × 2 block, and in front of an L corner's gable (`buildVillage.test.ts`).
- **Arches, paving, walkways**: an arch is a block with nothing below it, which a click can never create (clicks stack on top of the column), so arches are **automatic**, like roof caps: `syncAutoArches()` spans a street 1 or `MAX_ARCH_SPAN` (2) cells wide — never more, a wider street stays open, open at ground level, between two facing buildings that have a **wall** at the arch's level (occupied at y = 0, 1 and 2, so y = 1 is not their roof — an arch resting on the roofs of one-storey houses stood a full storey above their walls with its own roof), one arch per street stretch (consecutive rows with the same gap), in the middle; at a crossroads the X span wins. `recomputeProceduralLogic` runs roofs → arches → roofs: caps are up to date before supports are checked, and an arch that disappears (passage built) must leave room for that column's cap in the same pass — otherwise results depend on click order (a test builds the same shape in 50 random orders). `isSimpleArch` (the type rule) only requires one opposite pair and nothing below. Auto arches (`isAutoArch`) are not user blocks: not exported/saved, skipped by `getTopOccupiedY`/`getTopRealOccupiedY` (a click in the lane builds on the ground, demolition ignores them), and they vanish when their supports go. Test helpers note: `addBlockInColumn` fills gaps from the bottom; the UI path is `getNextPlacementY` + `addBlock`. An `Arch` cell is a solid masonry block whose underside is cut by a segmental arch, with voussoirs + keystone on both faces — no pillars. A multi-cell arch is one arc computed for the whole span (rise 0.42 / 0.66 for 1 / 2 cells): each cell draws its slice (`archBodyGeo(rise, span, slice)`) and the voussoirs whose middle falls in it (`archVoussoirMidX`); the span axis follows the neighbours on each side, the span length the contiguous `Arch` cells, and if nothing is built above, its top depends on the columns around all its cells: if one of its two supports has a gable roof at the arch-top level (y + 1), that roof continues over the arch **in that roof's ridge axis** (the span axis if a continuing roof follows it; else gables on both arch heads, if free) and butts against the other support — an arch roof across the support's ridge butted its open end against that roof's pan (stone merlons must never lean on tiles); else a crenellated walkway like curtain walls (`crenellatedWalkwayParts()`) if one is a tower column (`isTowerColumn`) **and** both supports have a wall at y + 1 (a walkway links two walls — not a spire's foot or thin air); else a gable roof, ridge from support to support (`gableRoofParts()`, exported from `roofCellParts`, `baseY: 1`), if **at least two** of those columns are topped by a gable roof (not a tower, not a rampart); else a plain flagstone deck (`walkwayDeckParts()`). A tower keeps its rounded corners all the way up (its base decides), so a flat arch abutting it would leave a vertical gap in each corner: `abutmentFillets()` fills them with `cornerFilletGeo()` (square minus quarter disc, same 10 arc segments as the tower contour), from the arch's foot to the top of its walkway/parapet, never inside the support (towers are hollow). Merlon arrow slits are real through-slits (`piercedMerlonParts()`: four blocks around the slit), not a dark box. Walkways use `flagstoneParts()` ([src/render/cells/walkwayParts.ts](../src/render/cells/walkwayParts.ts)). Ground: `buildVillage(…, ground)` paves every empty ground cell that touches a building, diagonals included ([src/render/cells/pavingParts.ts](../src/render/cells/pavingParts.ts)) — lanes, streets and passages under arches — plus any cell under an arch, with individual cobbles (`stoneBlockGeo` laid flat, 12 vertices, `rigidNormals`): regular courses across the street (`ROWS` 7 per cell), cobbles of varying length, staggered joints, slightly uneven heights and tints, about one cobble in five of every other row spanning two rows (`isBig`, same reason as the tall wall stones: unbroken row joints looked too aligned), all square and level (the former tilted, rotated boxes read as a pile of slabs). Like the wall facing, the layout lives in grid coordinates, so a cobble straddling two cells is drawn half by each and a street shows no cell boundary. Next to grass the paving frays: the bedding stops `FRINGE` short and ~40 % of the cobbles of that fringe are missing, grass showing between the others; further out the grass shows.
- **Tower propagation is intentional**: `isTowerColumn()` in `cellUtils.ts` propagates upward — if a block is isolated (no horizontal neighbours), all blocks below it in the column become cylindrical too. Don't simplify this to a per-block check; it's what makes towers look right. `isIsolatedBlock()` (used for roofs) is a separate, non-propagating check — this asymmetry is deliberate.
- **Colors**: base colors come from `settings.wallBaseColor` / `settings.roofBaseColor`; derived tones use `shades()` / `varyColorBrightness()` from `src/colorPalettes.ts`. Any per-cell variation must be deterministic (hash of coordinates) — never `Math.random()` in a builder.
- **Windows**: the "one window per floor between adjacent blocks" rule in `recomputeProceduralLogic()` only looks at neighbours already typed in the current pass (x-1, z-1), so the result depends on shape only, not click order. Keep it that way (there's a test for it).
- **Terrain generation** (`generateTerrain(gridSize, seed?)` in `villageGrid.ts`) uses seeded value noise ([src/utils/noise.ts](../src/utils/noise.ts)) for occupancy/height (clusters of buildings, ≥ 40 % of lots filled), empty rows as streets on grids ≥ 6, and rare +2 landmark columns. Same seed → same village (tested). Above 12 (`LARGE_GRID`), noise heights are capped at 6 blocks (landmarks still add 2), two isolated columns in three get a slightly lower neighbour (a small house), and two height peaks in three are cut to their tallest neighbour: a column taller than all its neighbours is a tower all the way down (`isTowerColumn`), so a 20 × 20 village used to be a forest of towers (~1 tower column per building, ~0.3 now). Grids up to 12 are generated exactly as before (tested). `sceneScale()` and `initialCameraScale()` also grow half as fast above 12, so a 20-grid village fills the starting view instead of a third of it.
- **Grid size & scene scale**: bounds live in [src/config/gridConfig.ts](../src/config/gridConfig.ts) (max 20 since the build moved to a worker with a per-building cache; beyond that, instanced stones and simplified distant buildings are needed — measure with `npm run perf`). Camera/fog distances were tuned for a 5-grid; `sceneScale()` stretches fog and `maxDistance`, `initialCameraScale()` the starting camera, and `shadowCameraHalfSize()` sizes the sun's orthographic shadow frustum (three's default ±5 only covered ~5 cells).
