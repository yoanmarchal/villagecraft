# Exterior: stone, roofs, openings

The outside of the buildings. Everything is procedural geometry: no texture and no UVs (merged geometries have none).

- **Stone walls are real geometry**: [src/render/cells/masonryParts.ts](../src/render/cells/masonryParts.ts) covers every wall face with stone blocks standing 1.4–2.4 cm proud of the wall (`ashlarStoneGeo(mask, width, height, thickness)`: a unit block scaled per stone, 12 vertices, cut like a quoin — a rounded arris of a fixed `ASHLAR_ROUND` (0.01, the quoins' `roundedBoxGeo` radius, whatever the stone's size: insets are given per stone as fractions snapped to a short geometric ladder, `ASHLAR_STEPS` — rounded to 0.01 they saturated the 1 500-geometry cache: evictions, new geometry ids, twice the chunks re-merged per click) and a flat face, normals turning from the side at the shoulder to straight ahead on the face; the former cushioned profile (`stoneBlockGeo`: arris proportional to the stone, face normals tilted to the edges) read as a wide chamfer and widened the joints — the user asked for the facing to look like the quoins, which must stay as they are; `stoneBlockGeo` is still used, unchanged, by paving, roof tiles and slates; such parts carry `rigidNormals`, so the merge applies the rotation only: the very uneven scale would flatten them). Two shader-only attempts were rejected by the user and must not come back: a lit bump stays flat when a wall is seen from the side, and parallax occlusion mapping twisted the stones without ever making them stand out. Castle bond, not brick: courses of unequal height (`courseBed`, `MASONRY_COURSES` 9 per storey, with a bed on every storey level so no stone straddles two storeys), stones of varying length, joints staggered from course to course (regular moulded bricks read as 19th-century), more than one stone in three of every other course is two courses tall (`TALL_ROWS`, `TALL_CHANCE`; the course above is interrupted there, never across a storey), and each stone is a little shorter than its course (`BED_SLACK`, per whole stone, so joints vary in thickness — tightened to 0.007 with a 0.006 `MASONRY_JOINT`, then to 0.0008 with a 0.0012 `STONE_JOINT` (~4 mm) when the arrises became rounder — the bed joints were dominated by `BED_SLACK` (up to twice the slack per bed), so tightening the joint alone changed nothing visible: the user wants the stones as close-jointed as possible; unequal courses and tall stones still keep the beds from running straight) — without these the beds ran as long straight lines from one end of a wall to the other, which the user twice found too aligned. **The layout lives in grid coordinates, not cell coordinates** — beds depend on height only, vertical joints on the abscissa along the wall — so two neighbouring cells of a building each draw their half of the same stone, with the same projection and tint (tested): merged buildings show no seam, and each cell stays independent for the chunk cache. A side of a piece that is an internal cut (cell boundary, arc segment) gets no joint, no flank and no normal tilt (`STONE_EDGE` mask). Per face, stones are clipped to the cell, cut around `holes` (window, door and threshold, slit — flush with the frames), raised above an arch's extrados (`floorAt`), and laid along the arc on rounded corners in short segments, each widened so that their fronts meet (they used to open as wedges). Faces that get stones: `masonryFacesOf()` — nothing built in front, **or only a roof** (a wall above a lean-to roof or a wall-walk is visible; it stayed bare when only `exposedFaces` were used). Arch cells face their two heads the same way, and with the same `masonryFacesOf()` rule (an arch head behind a lower roof stayed bare when `exposedFaces` was used). One joint for the facing, small works and voussoirs: `STONE_JOINT` (`geometryCache.ts`, ~4 mm; the voussoirs' was a fixed 0.012, ~4 cm). Quoins keep theirs, `QUOIN_JOINT` (0.006, `decorations.ts`): the user found them right as they were — don't touch the quoins when reworking the facing. Frames, quoins (`protrusion` 0.034) and voussoirs stand prouder than the stones — keep it so when adding wall decorations. The former scattered stones (`stoneParts`, `windowStonesPerFace`) are gone; `windowStoneRoughness` is now the stones' roughness. A generated 12-grid village is ~450 k vertices.
- **Small works & shader flags**: parapets, merlons (tower and wall-walk, pierced ones included) and chimney stacks are built in real stones too — `stoneCourseParts()` fills their volume with courses of rounded blocks that stand slightly proud of a mortar core, and a tower parapet reuses `masonryParts()` on its own contour (`extent`, `yRange`). `MaterialSpec.masonry` (part of `matKey`, `VC_MASONRY`) marks those cores, the hollow shell and the arch body: the shader only tints their exterior vertical faces as lime mortar, which shows between the stones; cavity faces (rooms, window reveals) stay plastered, same criterion as the interior light (`EXTERIOR_SUPPORT`).
- **Gable roofs are real geometry too**: each pan of `gableRoofParts` is a dark sheathing board covered with individual tiles (`stoneBlockGeo`, 12 vertices each, `rigidNormals`), laid in courses at constant height (`TILE_ROWS` per unit from the eave, so they line up across pans, cells and half-roofs), each tile tipped up at its lower edge over the course below, every other course shifted by half a tile, five tones of `roofBaseColor`. The painted tile pattern it replaced had jagged edges (the user called it a filter); the battens are gone. Spires get the same treatment (`towerRoofParts`): the `spireBandGeo` rings are a dark underlay, covered with courses of slates that go round the tower contour (`sampleContourPerimeter`), each slate set on the generatrix between two scales of the contour, tipped up at its lower edge, staggered from course to course; below `SLATE_MIN_SCALE` the tip stays bare under the finial (slates would overlap there). ~2.5 k vertices per spire. Gables are clad with vertical oak planks (their width is capped by the slope so the barge boards hide their stepped tops) over a dark backing.
- **Joinery & smooth contours**: doors are oak planks over a dark backing with wrought-iron strap hinges and a pull ring, window glazing bars are dark oak (`wallWindowCellParts.ts`; door tint varies per cell by coordinate hash). Extruded cell contours (`shapedBoxGeo`, the CSG cavity prism) get `toCreasedNormals(…, CONTOUR_CREASE_ANGLE)`: arc facets (9° apart) share normals, right angles stay sharp — with flat facets every tower showed vertical bands under the sky light.
- **Decorations & openings**: the stones of the wall facing are cut around openings by the `holes` each wall builder passes to `masonryParts()` (`openingHoles` in `wallWindowCellParts.ts`) — each builder keeps its own list, don't centralize it. `Wall` cells (`standardCellParts.ts`) have no trim strip any more: they are never on the ground floor, so their "plinth" ran along every storey line, and, straight, it stuck out of the rounded corners of towers (the user asked for it to go). Quoins are in [src/render/cells/decorations.ts](../src/render/cells/decorations.ts): a **continuous chain** up each salient corner of a building (no side neighbour) and up each side of a rounded junction's groove — never at a straight facade junction (they redrew the cell grid along long facades) nor in a re-entrant corner. One stone every two courses, laid on the facing's beds (`courseBed`; the storey's last stone takes three), separated by nothing but `QUOIN_JOINT`; on a square corner long and short in turn on each face (a harp, ≤ 0.18 so it stays clear of a window frame), on an arc a column of alternately wide and narrow stones (`arcQuoinAngle`). The user asked for enough corner stones for the wall and as few joints as possible: the former three 0.18-high blocks per storey left 14 cm of facing between them. `quoinHoles(ctx, face)` gives their footprints to the wall builders, which cut the facing around them like around openings. On a slope the chains go on down the plinth (`plinthQuoinStones()`, called by `plinthParts`): same courses below level 0, same alternation, down to the terrain at the foot of each corner, the last stone stopped just below it (the user asked for them on the walls below the floor too). Tested in `quoins.test.ts`: chain from floor to ceiling, harp, corners chosen, no facing stone inside a quoin, unbroken from the ground on a slope. [src/config/protectedAreasConfig.ts](../src/config/protectedAreasConfig.ts) and `isQuoinProtected()` date from the scattered stones that the facing replaced; nothing calls them any more.
